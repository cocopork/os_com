# EXT4 buffered cache pressure

User intent: native EXT4 buffered fio writes larger than guest RAM must progress through writeback rather than exhaust physical frames. Implement dirty-page backpressure and clean-page reclaim in the rebuilt kernel; do not change the NV research protocols.

Builder decisions:
- Track resident frames, dirty frames and in-flight writeback per filesystem through an optional shared PageCache accounting object. Charge dirty and writeback separately (conservative when a page is redirtied during writeback); release resident charge only at final frame destruction, including resize/drop and allocation failure.
- Native regular-file buffered I/O reserves capacity before acquiring inode locks or journal handles. Split large calls into at most 256 KiB chunks; budget the extra unaligned page. The default per-filesystem resident high watermark is 512 MiB, dirty high watermark 128 MiB, background dirty trigger 64 MiB. Background work also runs periodically for smaller dirty sets. These are initial engineering defaults, not measured optimal values.
- A weakly owned filesystem worker flushes bounded batches of cached regular-file pages, then reclaims clean pages. Never wait for journal commit while holding an inode lock or an admission reservation needed by the worker. Failed I/O remains dirty; expose writeback failure to waiting writers instead of silently accepting more data or hanging.
- Reclaim holds the VMO page-array lock, uses a nonblocking page lock, and removes only initialized clean frames with no writeback and exactly one reference (the array). Mapping/page-fault, buffered I/O, and BIO references therefore pin pages. Never use range invalidation as opportunistic reclaim. Pinned pages may prevent reclaim; fail admission with ENOMEM after a no-progress reclamation attempt rather than evict mapped data or wait forever.
- Cache accounting can cover all EXT4 backed pages; policy admission and periodic flushing target regular files. This is filesystem-local protection, not a general kernel OOM replacement or a complete mmap-dirty writeback implementation.
- No write-through on every syscall; background writeback keeps headroom and writers wait at high watermarks. Wait with no inode lock, journal handle or spinlock held. Weak worker ownership must permit filesystem teardown.

Acceptance: deterministic kernel tests cover accounting/repeated dirty transitions, writeback failure/redirty, clean reclaim with retained references, bounded batches, multi-inode pressure and content rereads. Build and existing EXT4/PageCache regressions pass. Re-run 4 KiB random and 128 KiB sequential buffered fio with 16 GiB ranges on the previously authorized /dev/nvme1n1p1 and 8 GiB guest RAM, keeping artifacts and reporting both fio and host device bandwidth.
